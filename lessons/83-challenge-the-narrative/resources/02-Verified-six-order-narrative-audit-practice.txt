# Narrative audit: lesson 83

Emmanuel Corels Academy

This is the exact fictional six-order teaching example from the saved lesson. It is not a result from a retailer or the course practice database. The data contains no customer, product category, inventory, profit or future-period fields.

Import `orders.csv` at `Orders!A1` for the main lesson's ISO month labels (`2025-04` and `2025-05`). The six order IDs and revenue amounts match the saved source example. April totals **400 dollars** and May totals **300 dollars**. May minus April is **-100**; dividing by April gives **-0.25**, or **-25%**.

For the Short's human-readable month display, `orders_display_labels.csv` contains the same IDs, months and amounts, with **April 2025** and **May 2025** written out. This changes no month or amount. The actual captured Google Sheets workbook uses these expanded labels. Its Evidence formulas are:

```text
=SUMIF(Orders!B2:B7,"April 2025",Orders!C2:C7)
=SUMIF(Orders!B2:B7,"May 2025",Orders!C2:C7)
```

If using the ISO file, use criteria `"2025-04"` and `"2025-05"`, as in the main lesson. Verify the actual results **400** and **300** before using the findings.

Create **Draft**, **Orders**, **Evidence** and **Trace** tabs. The unreviewed draft to challenge is:

> Company revenue fell 25% in May 2025, a $100 loss. Stockouts caused the decline. May had 20% fewer customers. Demand is collapsing, so revenue will keep falling. May revenue was $300. All categories performed worse.

Trace each numerical claim to its checked cell. Describe the population as these six example rows. A revenue decrease is not proof of financial loss. Remove causal, customer, category and future claims because the necessary fields are absent.

A defensible statement is:

> In this six-order example, recorded revenue totals $400 in April 2025 and $300 in May 2025. That is $100 lower, or 25% lower relative to April. These rows do not establish the cause of the change or whether the pattern holds outside this example.
